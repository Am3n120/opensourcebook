# Code of conduct

Open Sourcebook is a small project, and most people who come here are trying to learn something or to help. This page says how we treat each other in issues, pull requests, discussions and anywhere else the project lives.

## Be kind

- Assume the other person means well, and say what you mean plainly.
- Talk about the work, not the person. "This breaks when the file is empty" helps. "You clearly did not test this" does not.
- New people are welcome. A question that seems obvious to you was not obvious to the person asking it.
- It is fine to disagree, and it is fine to say no. Give your reason, and let it rest once a decision is made.

## What is not allowed

- Harassment of any kind: insults, threats, sexual remarks or unwelcome attention.
- Attacks on who someone is: their race, ethnicity, nationality, gender, sexuality, religion, disability, age or anything like these.
- Publishing someone's private details, such as an address or private messages, without their permission.
- Following someone from one thread to another to keep a fight going.
- Spam, and promotion that has nothing to do with the project.

## How to report

If something here was done to you, or you saw it done to someone else, tell the maintainer. Use GitHub's own report button on the comment or the profile (the ⋯ menu, then Report), which reports it privately. If you would rather it be seen in the open, open an issue that says only that you have a conduct problem to report, with no details, and a private way to talk will be arranged.

You do not have to be the person it happened to, and you do not have to answer the other person first.

## What happens

The maintainer reads every report and keeps who made it private. Depending on what happened, they may do nothing more than talk to the person, hide or remove the comment, lock the thread, or block the person from the project for a time or for good. Something serious or repeated leads to a block without a warning. One person maintains the project, so a reply can take a few days.

A decision can be questioned once, politely, by the person it was about. After that it stands.
