# How a video gets made: one worked session

The cookbook shows finished scenes. This shows the work between them: what one maker did on a 60-second video about how a siphon empties a tank. The topic has nothing to do with yours. What carries over is how the maker went back and forth between the words, the picture and what they saw. It is an example, not a procedure.

**Reading first.** The maker read every passage, then wrote down, for themselves, who was watching (someone who has seen a hose drain a pond and never knew why) and the one thing they should leave with (the water in the tube is pulled down by the longer side, so the tank empties uphill). That sentence went into about_video as the idea.

**First beat, first try.** The maker wrote the opening beat's words and a quick picture: a title and the words "a siphon" in a box. write_beat answered with the times of every word, a strip of frames and a line saying the picture was only words and boxes. The strip showed the same frame for nine seconds while the voice described a tank, a tube and a bucket. Nobody watching would see any of those three things.

**Drawing the thing.** The maker redrew the beat as a program: a tank on the left, a bucket lower on the right, a tube between them, each drawn as it appears on the word that names it ("tank" @3, "tube" @7, "bucket" @11). The new strip changed on those three frames and on no others. That was what they wanted.

**Letting the picture take its time.** In the second beat the water had to climb the short side of the tube and fall down the long side, and that took longer than the sentence about it. The maker added `[pause 1.5]` after "starts to flow", and the scene filled the tube during `@h1` to `@h1.end`. Then they looked at that stretch with look from 4 to 9 seconds. The water reached the top of the bend before the pause and sat still for most of it, so they started the fill later, at `@h1`, and looked again.

**A real picture where real mattered.** For the line about Roman aqueducts, the maker had picture tools in this job, so they searched the archives, found a photograph of a Roman inverted siphon, saved it with its credit, and put it beside the drawn tube. For the rest they kept the drawing, because a photograph of a hose would have hidden the idea behind the details of a garden.

**Slow first, fast after.** The first time water moved through the tube, every stage had its own words and its own frame. When the video came back to the tank emptying, a single phrase ran all of it at pace=fast, because the viewer already knew the move.

**Seeing the whole.** With five beats written, the maker used watch. Two cuts in a row opened on the same grey tank, so the video felt stuck. They rewrote the fourth beat to open close in on the bend of the tube. The total ran 74 seconds against the 60 asked for. They cut a sentence that only repeated the third beat, which brought it to 63.

**The real cut.** With every beat built, the maker rendered the video and went on choosing the sound while it ran. Watching the render through, with what the voice said under each frame, showed two things no single beat had: the music bed came in under the first line and covered "siphon", and the real voice ran a little faster than the estimate in the third beat, so the bucket arrived after the word that named it. They moved the bed to start on the second beat, nudged the bucket to its word, rendered again and watched that stretch.

**Finishing.** Each warning that was still there was one the maker had read and decided to keep: one label sat close to the bucket on purpose. The last render was the video as it stood, so that was what the person got.
