# Showing ideas working

A process described in words is heard and forgotten; the same process watched on a concrete instance, one step at a time, is understood. When a viewer needs to understand how something works or how to do it, the example you choose decides whether the idea can be seen at all.

Questions worth asking of each process the video teaches:

1. **Is there an instance at all?** Does the narration work through a concrete case, or only describe it?
2. **Does every step do real work in it?** An example where a step has nothing to do, a branch is never taken, or the case that makes the method necessary never comes up shows the wrong thing. A case where most steps are degenerate (one thing against one, nothing to carry, a single pass that already finishes) is correct and still hides the mechanism.
3. **Its size.** Most processes have a unit that repeats: a step per item, a round, a pass, a combining step. When that repetition is what the viewer must learn, a good example lets it happen in full at least twice: the first time slowly, the next ones faster. Small enough to follow by eye on a phone.
4. **Its values.** Easy arithmetic, values easy to tell apart, nothing the viewer must strain to compute while the next step arrives, unless the difficulty is the point.
5. **Can the picture show it truthfully?** Where a kit computes the process (sort, dijkstra, trace, matrix, det, cramer, matvec, math states), let it play the steps (`do steps ... at=@...`) so every step and label is right. Where none does, a program can run the real rule (see the guide on simulations).
6. **For a story or an argument,** the concrete instance is the evidence itself: the measurement, the document, the moment. Show it rather than summarize it.

Tempo follows understanding: the first time a step happens, give it its own moment on the words that say it; when it repeats, let it run faster under one phrase, then show the whole. A result appears after the work that produces it.

A demonstration costs a few more words than a description, usually paid for by the description it makes unnecessary.
