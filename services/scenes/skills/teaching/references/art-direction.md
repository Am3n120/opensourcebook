# One look across the video

Each scene is built on its own and the video cuts from one to the next, so what makes it feel like one piece is decided by you, across scenes. The style already sets colors, type and line. What the style alone cannot say:

- **Emphasis.** How the thing that matters now stands out, the same way every time: light on it while the rest stays, a push in with the camera, scale, a mark, color as meaning. And what is never used for emphasis in this video, so the viewer learns the code once.
- **Scale.** How big things are shown and how much fills the frame: one large subject edge to edge, a whole system small and then close-ups of its parts, real things at their true relative sizes on a stage. Change it on purpose, not by accident.
- **Motifs.** Shapes, objects, gestures or framings that recur and carry meaning: the same container every time a quantity is held, the same arrow for cause, the same corner for the running total. Draw a recurring thing the same way, in the same place, in each scene that has it, so the cut reads as "the same thing, later".
- **Texture.** The feel over time: calm and spacious, dense and quick, hand-made, clinical. Motion carries most of it (see How things move in the scene language).

**Across cuts.** Consecutive scenes that look alike make the cut invisible and the video feel stuck; consecutive scenes with nothing in common make it feel like slides. What usually works: when the next scene's first words build on something (they add to it, change it, run steps on it), open the next scene with that thing drawn where it was; when they turn to a new thread, cut to something that looks clearly different. Most joins carry nothing.

**Captions.** The narration is burned in as short caption pages near the bottom, in the style's font. The key words you give per beat (`emphasis` in write_beat) are drawn in the accent color as they are said. Pick the one or two words a viewer should hold on to in that scene (a term, a number, the turn of the argument), or none.
